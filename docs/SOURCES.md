# Sources and acknowledgements

## Darwin’s first edition

Charles Darwin. *On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection,
or the Preservation of Favoured Races in the Struggle for Life.*
London: John Murray, 1859.

- [Wellcome Collection record and digitized copy](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/ytv5whr6)
- [Wellcome IIIF manifest, b1802922x](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/presentation/v2/b1802922x)
- [Darwin Online first-edition text, F373](https://darwin-online.org.uk/content/frameset?itemID=F373&viewtype=text&pageseq=1)
- [Darwin Online first-edition images, F373](https://darwin-online.org.uk/content/frameset?itemID=F373&viewtype=image&pageseq=1)

Wellcome labels its digitized work with the Public Domain Mark. Credit:
*On the origin of species by means of natural selection, or, The preservation
of favoured races in the struggle for life / by Charles Darwin.*
Public Domain Mark. Source: Wellcome Collection.

Darwin Online’s matching first-edition text and images were used to reconcile
readings and reconstruct missing material. The source records identify the
Balfour and Newton Libraries, Cambridge, for the supplementary copy.

The reconstruction excludes the first four and last four scanned leaves,
removes duplicate scans of printed pages 42–43, and restores printed pages
294–295 and 324–325 together with missing preliminary matter. The 32-page
publisher’s catalogue is retained.

## Greek type references

The primary project reference is **Raphael Kühner’s 1859 Greek grammar**,
especially the alphabet on printed page 15 and repeated examples on pages
16–19 and 21–22. The packaged font metadata identifies this reference as
“Kuhner 1859.” The retained source records do not establish one definitive
catalogue URL for that particular scan, so a scan identifier is not guessed here.

Supplementary comparisons included Crosby’s grammar scan:

- [A Grammar of the Greek Language, Wikimedia-hosted scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/85/A_Grammar_of_the_Greek_Language_%28IA_agrammargreekla06crosgoog%29.pdf)

GFS Artemisia was used as a visual reference for the micro sign’s left
lower terminal. No Artemisia outline data were imported into the Origin fonts,
and the GFS fonts themselves are not distributed in this repository.

- [Greek Font Society: GFS Artemisia](https://www.greekfontsociety-gfs.gr/typefaces/20th_21st_century#GFS_Artemisia)
